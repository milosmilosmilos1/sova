Brand voice is **the way your business sounds in every post, message and reply**: whether you address customers formally or informally, whether you're serious or playful, which words you use and which ones you never use. When it's written down on one page, everyone who writes for you, person or AI, sounds the same. When it isn't, every post sounds as if someone else wrote it.

## Voice and tone are not the same thing

Your voice is your personality, and it doesn't change. Tone is how that personality sounds in different situations. A bakery can have a warm, playful voice. When it announces that it's closed because of a breakdown, the tone is more serious, but it's still the same bakery and it still sounds warm.

This matters because of the networks. On LinkedIn you'll be more serious than on TikTok. That's a change of tone. If on LinkedIn you sound like a different company, that's a change of voice, and customers notice it.

## Why does brand voice matter at all?

People buy from the businesses they recognise. When someone reads your post and thinks “this sounds like them” before they've even seen the profile name, you've done it. That only happens when every post sounds the same.

The second reason is practical. If your posts are written by you, your staff, an agency and an AI tool, without written rules you end up with four different brands. **One page of rules** fixes that in an hour.

## Six questions for a brand voice document

!slika:glas-brenda-na-drustvenim-mrezama-info

1. Who are we talking to? One sentence about the customer: who they are, how old they are, what matters to them.
2. Vi or ti? In Serbian this is **the first decision**, because the language has a formal “you” (vi) and an informal one (ti). Vi is more polite and the safer choice for services and companies. Ti feels closer and works for a younger audience, cafés and gyms.
3. Which three words describe us? For example “warm, expert, brief” or “cheerful, direct, local”.
4. Which words do we use? Product names, local expressions, technical terms customers understand.
5. Which words do we never use? Empty advertising phrases, jargon, words your customers don't like.
6. An example of a good post and a bad one. This is the most important item, because adjectives mean different things to different people, and an example doesn't.

Add a few practical rules as well: emojis or not, exclamation marks or not, Latin or Cyrillic script, and how the brand name is written.

## The same text in two voices

Look at how the same information sounds in two different voices. Both versions are correct. The question is which one is yours.

| Voice | Post about new opening hours |
|---|---|
| Polite, expert | “From 1 December we're also open on Saturdays, from 9:00 to 14:00. You can book an appointment by phone or by message.” |
| Warm, playful | “A Saturday without your hair done? Not with us. From 1 December we're open on Saturdays too, 9 to 2. Send us a message or give us a call, and we'll keep a spot for you.” |

Notice that both versions contain the same facts: the date, the day, the hours and how to book. **Voice changes how you sound, not what you say.**

## Brand voice in Serbian: decisions of its own

Serbian asks for a few decisions that English doesn't. They matter whenever you post in Serbian, for customers in Serbia or the wider region:

- Vi or ti, and whether to write Vi with a capital letter. Serbian has a formal “you” and an informal one, much like French vous and tu. In posts for a wide audience a lower-case “vi” is the norm, and a capital “Vi” is kept for addressing one person directly.
- Latin or Cyrillic. Serbian is written in both alphabets. You can use both, but consistently, by network or by type of post.
- Ekavian or Ijekavian, if you have customers from across the region. These are two standard variants of the language that spell some words differently, such as mleko and mlijeko for “milk”.
- Gender: how you address people when you don't know whether a man or a woman is reading. Serbian adjectives and past-tense verbs change with gender, and sentences like “zakažite termin” (“book an appointment”) avoid the problem.

You make these decisions once and write them into your rules. After that you don't have to think about them again. If you write in English, the closest equivalents are how formal you sound and whether you use British or American spelling.

## How do you stay the same on every network?

The personality stays, while the length, tone and format change:

- Instagram: shorter, more visual, with a clear call to action.
- Facebook: more conversational, a little longer, with an invitation to send a message.
- LinkedIn: a more serious tone, the story behind the business, your expertise. There's more on this in the article on [LinkedIn company pages](@linkedin-stranica-firme).
- TikTok: the most relaxed, spoken as if in conversation. Ideas are in the article on [TikTok for business](@tiktok-za-firme).

The check is simple: when you read posts from three networks one after another, **do they sound like the same person?**

## Brand voice and AI

AI tools write quickly, but without rules they sound like everyone else. That's why a written brand voice is the most important thing you can give an AI. The more specific it is, with examples, the closer the text gets to your own.

## How do you know the voice is working?

After a month, run two tests. First, read your last ten posts in a row. Do they sound like one brand? Second, ask two loyal customers to describe your profile in three words. If they use words similar to the ones you wrote down, the voice is working. If they don't, compare the two and add examples to your rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is brand voice?

The way your business sounds in posts, messages and replies: how you address people, your vocabulary, how serious you are, your humour. It stays the same on every network, while the tone adapts to the situation.

### Should I address customers formally (vi) or informally (ti)?

It depends on your audience. Vi, the formal form, is the safer choice for services, healthcare, companies and older audiences. Ti, the informal one, works for younger audiences, cafés and gyms. What matters is that the choice is the same in every post.

### How long should a brand voice document be?

One page is enough: six answers and two or three examples. Nobody reads a longer document, and a shorter one doesn't cover the basic decisions.

### Can AI write in my voice?

Yes, if you give it clear rules and examples. Without them it writes in a generic voice that sounds like an ad for anyone at all.
