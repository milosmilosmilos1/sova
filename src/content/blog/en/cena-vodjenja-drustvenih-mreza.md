The cost of managing social media is essentially **the cost of 15 to 20 hours of work a month** for two networks and about twelve posts. If you do it yourself, you pay with your time. If a freelancer or an agency does it, you pay for those hours at their rate, plus visuals and reports. A tool like Sova cuts the number of hours, and the cost with it, but it doesn't remove the need for someone to look over the posts and approve them.

## What does the work consist of?

When someone says “managing social media”, they usually mean six jobs. Each one takes time, and each one can be charged for separately.

1. Strategy and a monthly plan: what gets posted, when and why.
2. Text for every post, adapted to each network.
3. Visuals: photos, graphics, carousels, short video.
4. Publishing or scheduling at the right time.
5. Comments and messages, every day.
6. A monthly report: what worked and what didn't.

Many offers leave out the last two items. Ask explicitly who replies to messages and what goes into the report.

## How do you work out the cost for your own case?

Multiply the hours by the hourly rate. If someone works for €10 an hour, 18 hours comes to **€180 a month**. At €25 an hour, the same workload comes to **€450 a month**. On top of that, add any on-site shooting and the ad budget.

The number of hours grows with each network, but not in a straight line. A second network doesn't take twice the work, because the same idea gets adapted, not invented all over again. The third and fourth networks add a few hours a month each. How many posts you really need is covered in the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## Four ways, four prices

| Option | What you pay for | What to watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Yourself | your own time, 15 to 20 hours a month | when the business gets busy, the posts stop |
| Freelancer | hours at the agreed rate | what happens when they're on holiday or ill |
| Agency | a monthly package, often with a report | how many posts and networks the package really includes |
| AI tool | a monthly subscription | how much time you have left for reviewing and photos |

No single option is always best. A small bakery with one owner and a clear tone often does best on its own, with a tool that cuts down the writing. A company with five services in two cities probably needs someone who thinks about it every day.

## What should the offer spell out?

A good offer is specific. Before you sign, check that it states the following:

- Which networks, and how many posts a month on each.
- Who makes the photos and video, and whether they come to your premises.
- Who replies to comments and messages, and how quickly.
- Whether you approve posts before they go out, and how.
- What exactly goes into the monthly report.
- How long the contract runs and how it can be ended.
- Whether the ad budget is included or paid separately.

## What should make you suspicious?

A promise of a specific number of followers is a bad sign. Followers can be bought, and bought followers don't buy anything. They also drag down the reach of your real posts. The same goes for “guaranteed reach” without ads, because nobody controls the algorithm.

The second sign is an agency that opens accounts in its own name. **The accounts must be yours**, and the agency gets access as an admin or editor. If the partnership ends, the profile, the followers and the history stay with you.

The third sign is a report with thirty charts and not a single recommendation. A report should tell you what worked and what changes next month. What's worth tracking is explained in the article on [social media analytics](@analitika-drustvenih-mreza).

## How do you cut costs without losing quality?

First, cut the number of networks, not the number of posts on your main network. Two well-run networks are worth more than four half-run ones. Second, make a plan for the whole month in one go, as described in the article on [building a content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze). Third, shoot your photos in batches, once a week, instead of whenever you need one.

Fourth, hand the repetitive parts over to a tool: first drafts of captions, adapting posts for each network, scheduling. Your time goes on the things only you know. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is the price per post or per month?

Usually per month, for an agreed number of posts and networks. A price per post makes sense for one-off jobs, such as a campaign before an opening, but for ongoing management a monthly agreement is clearer.

### Is the ad budget included in the management fee?

Almost never. Ad money goes directly to Meta, Google or TikTok from your card, and management is a separate service. If an offer says otherwise, ask how much of the amount actually goes on ads.

### How much does it cost just to create posts, without publishing them?

Less, because comments, messages and reports drop out. But be careful: posts with nobody answering the comments lose their point, because customers' questions go unanswered.

### Is it worth paying annually?

It's worth it when you're sure you'll stick with the same way of working. With tools, an annual subscription is usually much cheaper per month. With agencies, ask for a trial period of two to three months before you sign an annual contract.
