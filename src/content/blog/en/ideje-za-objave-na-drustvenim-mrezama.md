The best post ideas come from your everyday work: **customer questions, how your product is made, the people who work for you and the results you get for clients**. Below are 40 concrete ideas: ten for any business, plus seven or eight each for four types of business. Pick five, put them in next month's plan and get going.

:::ukratko
- The best source of ideas is the questions customers ask you every day.
- **Behind the scenes** posts and posts about your team almost always do better than advertising.
- Any idea can be a photo, a carousel, a short video or a story.
- Make sales posts one in three or four, not every post.
- Repeat ideas that did well after a few months, in a new format.
:::

## Ten ideas for any business

1. The answer to the question customers ask you most often.
2. What the morning before opening looks like.
3. Meet the team: one person, what they do and what they love about the job.
4. Before and after: the result of your work, with the customer's permission.
5. A customer's impression, copied from a message or review, with their permission.
6. A mistake customers often make and how to avoid it.
7. Something new on offer, with the price and how to order.
8. A number from your business: how many coffees a day, how many years you've been open, how many customers you've served.
9. A local recommendation: the shop next door, an event in town, a place you love.
10. A question for your audience: which of two new things should you introduce?

These ten cover all three basic content groups: your offer, the story behind the business and useful information. How to spread them across the month is explained in the article on [content pillars](@stubovi-sadrzaja).

## Cafés, restaurants and bakeries

1. How the dish of the day is made, in a 20-second video.
2. Who your baker or chef is and how long they've been with you.
3. The weekend menu, posted on Thursday.
4. Ingredient of the week: where it comes from and why you chose that one.
5. Your best-selling dish and why people order it.
6. The coffee you make differently from everyone else.
7. Holiday bookings, with the last date to book.
8. A local supplier: the farm, bakery or brewery you work with.

Food is the most rewarding subject to photograph, but only when the picture is good. Daylight by a window and a phone held overhead solve most problems. There's more on this in the article on [social media for restaurants and cafés](@drustvene-mreze-za-restorane-i-kafice).

## Salons, gyms, studios and clinics

1. Before and after, with a short explanation of what was done.
2. A myth from your profession and what the truth is.
3. Free slots for this week, in a story.
4. How to prepare for a first visit.
5. A piece of equipment or a product you use, and why.
6. A day in the life of a team member, from morning to closing time.
7. An expert's answer to a question from the comments.
8. A challenge for followers: seven days, one small habit.

With services, **people buy trust**. The face and name of the person who does the work, shown with their consent, build more trust than any graphic.

## Shops and online sales

1. The product in use, not just on a white background.
2. Three ways to combine a single product.
3. Opening a delivery of new stock.
4. How an order gets packed.
5. A comparison of two similar products and who each one suits.
6. The most common question about delivery and returns, answered clearly.
7. A photo a customer sent you, with their permission.

For online sales, **a short video of the product in someone's hand** works better than a studio photo, because it shows the size and the material. Ideas for clips are in the article on [Reels ideas for local businesses](@ideje-za-reels-za-lokalni-biznis).

## Services for companies (accountants, IT, agencies, consultants)

1. A deadline clients often forget and how not to miss it.
2. A case study: the problem, what you did, the result in numbers.
3. A change in regulations or tools, explained in five sentences.
4. What working with you looks like, step by step.
5. A question to ask before choosing a supplier in your field.
6. A lesson from a mistake, yours or someone else's, without naming the client.
7. A new team member and what they bring to clients.

These topics work best on LinkedIn, as a short story or a carousel. How to run a company page is covered in the article on [LinkedIn company pages](@linkedin-stranica-firme).

## How do you turn one idea into five posts?

One good idea isn't used up by a single post. Take “a mistake customers often make”:

- a carousel with five steps for avoiding it,
- a short video where you show the mistake and the fix,
- a story with a poll: “has this ever happened to you?”,
- a LinkedIn post with a story from your own work,
- an update on your Google Business Profile with a tip and a call to action.

That's five posts for two weeks, and only one idea. **Adapting for each network** is also the biggest part of the work that Sova takes over: from one topic it writes text that follows each network's rules and makes a visual in your brand colours. ## How do you spread ideas across the month?

A rule that works for most small businesses: out of every four posts, **one sells and three give something**. A tip, a story, something to smile at, a piece of information. People follow profiles they get something from, and those are the profiles they buy from, too.

Put your ideas in a monthly plan, alternate the types of post (photo, carousel, video) and leave room for whatever comes up along the way. The process is described in the article on [building a content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze), and how many posts are enough is covered in the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## When ideas run dry

It will happen. When it does, one of these helps:

- Go through your messages and comments from the last three months and pull out the questions.
- Look at your three best posts from last year and make a new version of each.
- Ask someone on your team what customers ask them most often.
- Walk around your shop with your phone and photograph ten small details customers never see.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I post when I have no ideas?

The answer to a question a customer asked you this week. It's the safest topic, because you know someone is interested. If you don't even have that, show what your work looks like behind the scenes.

### How many sales posts is too many?

If every other post is an ad, that's too many. A good ratio is one sales post for every three or four that give something: a tip, a story or a piece of information.

### Can I repeat an old post?

You can, after a few months and in a new format. Most of your followers never saw the old post, and the ones who did will be glad to see it again if it was useful.

### Are trends and challenges good for a small business?

Sometimes. A trend that fits your business and your tone can bring you reach. A forced trend that has nothing to do with what you do usually brings a few likes and not a single customer.
