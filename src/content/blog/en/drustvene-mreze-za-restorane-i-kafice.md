For a restaurant or café, most guests come from **Instagram with good photos of the food and the space, Google Business Profile with accurate opening hours and reviews, and Facebook for events and the local crowd**. Post three times a week and put the daily specials in Stories. What matters most is that the picture makes people hungry and that a guest knows straight away how to book or get there.

:::ukratko
- Instagram, Google Business Profile and Facebook are the basics for hospitality.
- **A real photo of your own food**, in daylight, is worth more than any stock image.
- Daily specials go in Stories, and dishes, the space and the people go in posts.
- Booking must take one tap: a message, a call or a link.
- Accurate opening hours and replies to reviews are a must, not an option.

## Which networks, and why?

**Instagram is your shop window.** People look at the food, the space and the atmosphere before they decide where to go. A profile with lovely photos of dishes and full tables sells better than any ad.

Google Business Profile is where the decision gets made. “Restaurant near me”, “café with garden Dorćol” (a Belgrade neighbourhood), “breakfast Novi Sad”: the people typing these are looking for a place right now. Opening hours, photos, the menu and reviews on your profile decide it. How to run it is covered in the article on [Google Business Profile posts](@google-poslovni-profil-objave).

Facebook is good for events, local groups and an older audience. If you put on live music, tastings or parties, a Facebook event still works.

TikTok and Reels are for places that have something to show in motion: cooking, cocktail making, latte art. They're not a must, but they can bring in lots of new people from around town.

## Photographing food on your phone

You don't need a photographer for every post. You need a few habits:

- Daylight, a table by the window, no flash.
- Two angles almost always work: from above for plates and bowls, from the side for burgers, cakes and drinks.
- A clean plate and a clean table. A crumb on the rim shows up more in a photo than it does at the table.
- Take the photo straight away, before the dish loses its shine.
- People in the frame, such as a hand holding a cup or cutting into something, bring the picture to life, but only with permission.

**Stock photos of food are a bad idea.** A guest comes in, gets a different-looking plate and feels cheated. Always show your own.

## What to post: a sample week

!slika:drustvene-mreze-za-restorane-i-kafice-info

A bistro in Kragujevac, with three posts a week plus Stories every day.

| Day | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Instagram Stories | dish of the day and its price |
| Tuesday | Instagram and Facebook | short video: how the dish of the week is made |
| Thursday | Instagram, Facebook, Google | weekend menu and bookings |
| Saturday | Instagram | photo of a full room or the team |
| Every day | Instagram Stories | daily specials, free tables, guests (with permission) |

Thursday is the day for the weekend, because that's when people plan where they'll go on Friday and Saturday, and book a table while there's still room. Monday and Tuesday are quieter, so they're good for a story about a dish and the kitchen. ## Your menu on social media

Your menu should be easy to find. People ask “what do you have?” and “how much is it?” more often than anything else. Good solutions:

- Instagram Highlights on your profile with the menu by category,
- the menu on your Google Business Profile, with prices,
- a photo of the daily specials in Stories every morning.

Avoid a menu that's a blurry photo of a sheet of paper, or a PDF that won't open on a phone. If your prices change, change them everywhere on the same day. **An old price on social media is the most common cause of a bad impression** at the very start of a visit.

## Bookings and messages

Every post should have **a clear next step**: “Book by message”, “Call 064 ...”, “Booking link in our bio”. If a guest has to hunt for your number, they'll go to someone who gave them theirs.

Reply to messages quickly: within a couple of minutes during opening hours, and by the next morning outside them. An automatic reply with your opening hours and a booking link helps when you're busy. Reply to comments under your posts within the first hour, because that's when a post gets the most attention.

## Reviews

For restaurants and cafés, Google reviews count almost as much as photos. Ask happy guests for one, on the bill, with a QR code on the table or through a word from the waiter. Reply to every review: thanks for praise, calm and specific answers to criticism. Guests read the replies as much as the reviews, because they show how you behave when something goes wrong. Don't delete or report bad reviews just because they're bad. People notice, and it looks worse than the criticism itself.

## The most common social media mistakes in hospitality

- Wrong opening hours on Google, especially over the holidays.
- Nothing but promotions and discounts, with no dishes and no people.
- Dark photos taken in the evening under yellow light.
- A menu that can't be found or is out of date.
- A profile that goes quiet for a month and then posts five things in one day.
- Posts with no call to book and no address.

A rhythm of three posts a week is easier to keep up than it looks, especially if you write all the captions in one go. The numbers for each network are in the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which social networks are best for a restaurant?

Instagram and Google Business Profile are the basics, and Facebook helps with events and the local crowd. TikTok is an extra for places that have time for short videos.

### How often should a restaurant post?

Three posts a week and Stories almost every day. Post the weekend menu on Thursday, when people are planning their night out.

### How do I photograph food on my phone?

In daylight by a window, from above for plates and from the side for burgers and cakes, without flash and straight after the food is served. Clean the table and the rim of the plate before you take the photo.

### Should I post prices?

Yes. Guests look for prices before they come, and a clear price saves time for them and for your staff. Just make sure the prices on social media are the same as on the menu.
