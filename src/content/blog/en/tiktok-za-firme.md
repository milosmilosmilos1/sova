TikTok makes sense for a business when **your product or service can be shown in a short video**: how it's made, how it's used, what the result looks like. You don't have to dance or follow every trend. To start, all you need is a business account, three videos a week shot on your phone and two to three months of patience.

## Is TikTok right for your business?

Ask yourself three questions. Can people see what you do? A bakery, a salon, a workshop, a shop, a gym and a restaurant all have something to show. Do your customers watch short videos? These days people in their 40s and 50s do too, not just the young. Do you have someone who will film three times a week? Without that, even the best idea won't help.

If the answers are yes, yes and yes, TikTok is worth a try. If you sell complex services to businesses, such as bookkeeping or software, LinkedIn is usually the better first choice. It's covered in the article on [the LinkedIn company page](@linkedin-stranica-firme).

## How does TikTok decide who sees your video?

TikTok first shows every new video to a small group of people, then watches how they react: do they watch to the end, do they watch it again, do they comment, share and save? If they react well, it shows the video to a bigger group. That's why even a profile with zero followers can have a video with thousands of views.

For a business, this means two things. First, **every video is a new opportunity**, however many followers you have. Second, **the first second and keeping people watching to the end** matter more than anything else.

## First steps

1. Open an account and switch it to a business account in the settings. You get analytics and the option of a link on your profile.
2. Write a short bio: what you do, where, and one call to action (“orders by message”).
3. Before your first post, watch twenty or so videos from similar businesses to get a feel for the rhythm and tone.
4. Film five videos in advance, then post three a week. Having a reserve stops you from going quiet.
5. After a month, look at your analytics to see which videos held people's attention longest.

## What should you film?

The videos that do best look as if a person filmed them, not an advertising agency:

- the process, from raw materials to finished product, sped up,
- before and after,
- an answer to a question from the comments, said to camera,
- a day in the shop, in ten short shots,
- a mistake customers make and how to avoid it,
- packing an order or getting ready to open.

## TikTok without dancing and without your face

You don't have to appear on camera if you don't want to. Many successful business profiles show only hands and the product, or the space, with on-screen text and original sound. The sound of a coffee grinder, a pair of scissors or an oven is interesting enough.

Still, a face and a voice build trust faster. If someone on your team likes to talk, let them be the voice of the profile. **People follow people**, not logos.

Agree on just one thing: who films and who replies to comments. If that's two different people, make sure they keep each other in the loop. Customers notice when a question under a video sits for days without an answer, and TikTok notices when the conversation under a video is alive.

## How often should you post?

To start with, **three to five videos a week**. TikTok rewards regularity and gives every video a fresh chance, so posting more often usually brings more views, as long as the quality holds up. One good video is worth more than three rushed ones. How to set a rhythm you can keep up is covered in the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## Captions, hashtags and sound

A TikTok caption can be long, but a short one works better: one sentence that adds to the video, plus three to five hashtags, at least one of them with your location. TikTok search reads the caption, the on-screen text and what's said in the video, so use the words people type when they're searching: “custom cake Novi Sad”, not just the name of your cake.

Business accounts have access to a limited library of music cleared for commercial use. Popular songs that personal accounts use are often not available. That's not a big problem, because original sound and your own voice work very well.

## Common mistakes businesses make on TikTok

- Reposting an ad from television or Facebook. People skip a video that looks like an ad within the first second.
- A logo at the start. The first second has to show something interesting, not your logo.
- Videos with another app's watermark. The networks show them to fewer people.
- Giving up after two weeks. TikTok takes time, and the first few months are a learning period for you and for your audience.
- Ignoring comments. Your reply to a comment can be your next video.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is TikTok only for young people?

No. Every generation watches short videos these days, and TikTok shows each video to people who might like it. For a local business, that includes people in your own town.

### Do I have to appear in the videos?

No. Hands, the product, the space and on-screen text are enough. A face and a voice do build trust faster, though, so if someone on your team likes being on camera, make use of it.

### How long does it take to see results on TikTok?

Count on two to three months of regular posting. A single video can do very well in the very first week, but steady growth takes time.

### Should I open a business account or a personal one?

For a company, a business account. You get analytics and a link on your profile, with the restriction that you use music cleared for commercial use.
