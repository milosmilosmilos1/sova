Google Business Profile posts are **short updates, offers and events that people see when they search for you on Google Search and Maps**. A post can be up to 1,500 characters long, with a photo and a button such as “Call now” or “Book”. For a local business, one post a week is enough to make the profile look alive, and the people who find you there are usually ready to buy straight away.

## Why does your Google profile matter, and why is it so often forgotten?

When someone types “bakery Liman” (a neighbourhood in Novi Sad) or “hairdresser near me” into their phone, Google shows a map and a few profiles. These people aren't scrolling out of boredom. They're looking for somewhere to go right now. That's why a profile with up-to-date opening hours, good photos, reviews and fresh posts is often where the decision gets made.

Even so, many businesses set up a profile once and forget about it. The last post is two years old, the opening hours are out of date, the reviews have no replies. That's why this is a network where **a little effort gives you a big advantage** over the competition.

## Three types of post

!slika:google-poslovni-profil-objave-info

An update is a general post: a new service, new opening hours, a product photo, a tip. It's the most common type and the one you should post regularly.

An offer has a title, a validity period and, if you like, a code or terms. It's good for time-limited promotions, for example “10% off haircuts on Tuesdays in December”. It stays visible until the end of the period.

An event has a title and a start and end date and time. A workshop, a tasting, an opening, an open day. It's visible until the day of the event.

An ordinary update is most prominent for a short time after you post it, so **consistency matters more than volume**. One update every week means there's always something fresh on your profile.

## What should you write in a post?

The rules are similar to Instagram, with one difference: people on Google are looking for information, not entertainment. Write practically.

1. The first sentence says the main thing: what's new and why it matters to the customer.
2. The second and third sentences give the details: price, deadline, how to order.
3. The button leads to an action that makes sense: a call, a booking, an order or your website.
4. The photo is real, taken in your own space, and well lit.

Only the start of the text shows in the preview, so the key information goes at the beginning. Hashtags serve no purpose here, so leave them out. An example: “From Monday we're also baking spelt bread, every morning from 7:00. Price: 260 RSD. Call us and we'll put one aside for you.” Short, useful, with a reason to call.

## Photos on your profile

Photos on your profile don't live only in posts. Google also shows the photos of your premises, products and team that you add to the profile, as well as the ones your customers add. Add new photos at least once a month: the shop window, the interior, your products, the people who work there (with their consent). The photo people see on the map often decides whether they click on you or on the place next door.

## Reviews and replies

Reviews aren't posts, but they do the same job: they show that the profile is alive and that someone cares. Reply to every one, briefly and personally. When someone praises you, thank them and mention something specific from their review. When someone criticises you, reply calmly, admit what's true and offer a solution, without arguing in public.

Ask happy customers for a review. The easiest moment is right after the service, with a sentence and a link, or with a QR code on the receipt. Don't offer a reward for a review and don't buy reviews, because that breaks Google's rules and can put your profile at risk.

## Opening hours and holidays

The most common mistake on a Google profile isn't a bad post. It's the wrong opening hours. A customer turns up, the door is locked, and Google said “open”. A customer like that rarely gives you a second chance.

Before every holiday, enter special opening hours: New Year, Orthodox Christmas (7 January), Easter, Labour Day (1 May), and your slava (the feast of the patron saint a family or business celebrates) if you close that day. **Post an update as well** with the same information, so people see it in your posts too.

## How often should you post?

For most local businesses, **one post a week** is the right amount, plus offers and events when you have them. That's four to five posts a month, about fifteen minutes a week. ## Do posts help you rank higher on Maps?

Google doesn't publish the exact recipe for ranking on Maps. What is known is that three things matter: distance from the person searching, how well the profile matches the search, and how well known and well rated the business is. Posts on their own are no magic wand, but an active, complete profile, with photos, reviews and replies, helps people choose you when they see you. And that's what counts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many characters can a Google Business Profile post have?

Up to 1,500 characters. Only the beginning shows in the preview, so the most important information goes in the first sentence.

### How long does a post stay visible?

An offer stays visible until the end of the period you set, and an event until the date of the event. An ordinary update is most prominent for a short time after posting, so it's a good idea to post once a week.

### Should I put hashtags on my Google profile?

No. Hashtags on Google Business Profile have no function. Write practically: what's new, the price, the deadline and how people can contact you.

### Do posts affect where I appear on Google Maps?

Google doesn't publish the exact recipe. An active, complete profile with photos, reviews and posts helps people choose you, but the most important factors are distance, how well you match the search and the business's reputation.
