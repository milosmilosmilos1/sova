Content pillars are **the three to five recurring topics your business posts about**, for example products, behind the scenes, tips and special offers. Every post belongs to one pillar. That way you plan faster, your profile doesn't turn into nothing but advertising, and at the end of the month you can see which topic brings in the most reactions and messages.

:::ukratko
- Three to five pillars are enough. Any more and your content gets spread too thin.
- Pillars come from two sources: **what you want to sell** and what customers want to know.
- Keep the sales pillar to a quarter of your posts at most.
- Any pillar can be a photo, a carousel, a video or a story.
- Measure results by pillar, not just by post.
:::

## Why do you need pillars?

Without pillars, every post is a new decision. A special offer today, holiday greetings tomorrow, another offer the day after. After two months the profile looks like a flyer, and people stop looking.

With pillars you have a framework. When you make your plan, you don't ask yourself “what should I post?” but “which pillar is up next, and what's the new topic within it?”. That's a much easier question. What a full monthly plan looks like is explained in the article on [building a content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze).

## How do you choose your pillars?

Start with two lists.

The first list: what you want to achieve. More bookings on Tuesdays? Sales of a new service? People knowing that you deliver? Each goal suggests a topic.

The second list: what customers ask you. Write down the ten questions you hear most often. They usually fall into two or three topics, and those are your useful pillars.

Then bring the two lists together. A pillar has to meet at least one condition: it helps your goal or it answers a real customer need. The best pillars meet both.

## Example pillars for four types of business

| Business | Pillars |
|---|---|
| Bakery | products, behind the scenes (how it's made), tips and recipes, offers and holidays |
| Hair salon | before and after, hair care at home, the team, free slots |
| Clothing shop | new stock, how to put outfits together, customers wearing our clothes, offers |
| IT company | case studies, tips for business owners, team and culture, industry news |

Notice that in each example **at most one pillar sells directly**: offers or free slots. The product pillars show what's on offer, and the others build trust, show expertise or show the people behind the business. Whether a sales post works depends on exactly those posts.

## How many posts per pillar?

A simple starting split for four pillars:

- useful content (tips, answers): about 30%,
- product or service: about 30%,
- behind the scenes and people: about 25%,
- offers and sales calls to action: about 15%.

If you post three times a week, that's roughly twelve posts a month: four tips, four about the product, three behind the scenes and one or two offers. These numbers aren't set in stone. After two months, look at the results and shift the percentages towards the pillar that works best.

## A pillar is not a post format

A common mix-up: “Reels” isn't a pillar, and neither is “carousel”. A pillar is a topic, and the format is the way you show it. The pillar “hair care at home” can be a five-step carousel, a short video, a story with a poll or a post with a product photo. Alternate formats within a pillar, and your profile stays varied. Formats are covered in the articles on [carousels](@instagram-karusel) and [Reels ideas](@ideje-za-reels-za-lokalni-biznis).

## Campaigns: temporary pillars

Alongside your permanent pillars there are campaigns, topics with a start and an end. Opening a new location, a holiday offer, a new product. A campaign runs for a few weeks and during that time takes some of your slots, say one post a week. When it ends, the slots go back to the permanent pillars.

A campaign still has to respect the balance between selling and useful content. A month in which every post says “buy a gift voucher” wears your audience out before the holidays even arrive.

## The most common mistakes with pillars

The first mistake is having too many pillars. With eight of them, each gets one post a month and none of them makes an impression. People remember a business for two or three things, not eight. If you can't decide, merge similar ones: “tips” and “frequently asked questions” are usually the same pillar.

The second mistake is pillars that are really one pillar. If a clothing shop has the pillars “new stock”, “sale” and “offers”, all three say the same thing: buy. The profile then looks like a catalogue. **At least one pillar has to be about people**, and at least one about something useful for the customer that isn't your product.

The third mistake is a pillar you can't keep up. “Recipe of the week” sounds great, but if you don't have time to cook and take photos every week, the pillar disappears after a month. **Choose topics where the material already exists** in your everyday work, because then the posts practically make themselves.

The fourth mistake is writing the pillars down and forgetting about them. Put them in a column of your plan, next to each post, and you'll see at a glance whether the month is balanced or there are three offers in a row. **A pillar in your plan works, a pillar in a drawer doesn't.**

## How do you measure pillars?

At the end of the month, add up the results by pillar, not just by post: average reach, saves and shares, messages. You'll often find that one pillar keeps winning, for example “behind the scenes”. That's a signal to give it more room. Replace a pillar that brings in nothing three months in a row with a new one. ## Frequently asked questions

### What are content pillars?

The recurring topics a business posts about on social media, usually three to five. Every post belongs to one pillar, which makes planning and measuring easier.

### How many pillars should I have?

Three to five. With fewer than three your profile gets monotonous, and with more than five it's hard to post regularly in each one.

### Do pillars have to be the same on every network?

No, but they're usually similar. On LinkedIn the expertise pillar may take the lead, and on Instagram the behind the scenes one. What matters is that the brand's personality stays the same.

### How often should I change my pillars?

Rarely. Review them every three months and replace the one that keeps doing poorly. Pillars are the foundation, so you don't change them every month.
