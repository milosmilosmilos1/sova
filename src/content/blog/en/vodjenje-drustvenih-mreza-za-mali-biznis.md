For a small business, managing social media means **two to three posts a week on the two networks where your customers are**, written in your own voice, published when people are watching, with one look at the numbers each month. You don't need all seven networks and you don't need to post every day. You need a rhythm you can keep up for six months.

:::ukratko
- Start with two networks: where your customers are, not where the crowd is biggest.
- Make a **plan for the whole month** in one sitting, in an hour or two, instead of coming up with a post every morning.
- Three types of post cover almost everything: product or service, behind the scenes, useful information.
- Once a month, look at reach, engagement and messages, then change one thing.
- A tool or AI can take over part of the work, but approval stays with you.
:::

## Why does a small business need to be on social media at all?

Customers check you out before they come. They look at Instagram to see what the place looks like, at Google to see your opening hours and ratings, at Facebook to see whether you're still open. A profile whose last post is a year old tells them nobody is minding the details.

The second reason is habit. People buy from the businesses they remember. A bakery that posts every morning at 7:00 to say the croissants are out of the oven doesn't need thousands of followers. It's enough that you think of it when you walk down that street.

## Which networks should you choose?

Start with the customer, not the network. The table below is a rough split that holds for most small businesses in Serbia.

| Type of business | First two networks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Café, restaurant, bakery | Instagram and Google Business Profile | photos of food, and people looking for somewhere nearby |
| Salon, gym, studio | Instagram and Facebook | bookings by message and local groups |
| Services for companies (accountant, IT, agency) | LinkedIn and Google Business Profile | owners and managers make the decisions |
| Online shop | Instagram and TikTok | short video sells the product |
| Tradespeople and repair services | Google Business Profile and Facebook | people search urgently and locally |

If you're not sure, ask your last ten customers how they heard about you. If three of them say “through Instagram”, you have your answer. How to get found by people searching for a service nearby is covered in the article on [Google Business Profile posts](@google-poslovni-profil-objave).

## How many posts a week is enough?

For a small business, **two to three posts a week per network** is a good start. That's eight to twelve posts a month, enough for people and the algorithm to see that you're active, but not so many that the business suffers. Regularity matters more than volume: three posts every week work better than ten in one week followed by a month of silence. The numbers for each network are in the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## What should you post?

Most small businesses can work with three types of post that take turns:

1. Product or service: what you sell, what it costs, how to order.
2. Behind the scenes: who works there, how the product is made, what the morning before opening looks like.
3. Useful information: a tip, an answer to a common question, a short lesson from your trade.

These groups are called content pillars, and there is a [separate article on content pillars](@stubovi-sadrzaja). When you run out of ideas, there's a [list of 40 post ideas](@ideje-za-objave-na-drustvenim-mrezama), sorted by type of business.

## How should you organise the work?

The biggest mistake is coming up with a post on the day. Then every post takes an hour, because first you hunt for an idea, then a photo, then the text. It's better to work in batches:

1. Once a month, in an hour or two, make a **content plan** for the whole month: date, network, topic, type of post.
2. Once a week, film or photograph everything you need for that week.
3. Write all the captions in one go and schedule them.
4. Set aside ten minutes every day for comments and messages.

You'll find what a good monthly plan looks like, with an example, in the article on [building a social media content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze). Scheduling saves the most time, and the process is described in the article on [scheduling posts on Instagram and Facebook](@zakazivanje-objava-na-instagramu-i-facebooku).

## How do you write so it sounds like you?

Customers recognise template text. “Visit us and see the quality for yourself” says nothing. “The croissants come out of the oven at 7:00, come while they're warm” says everything. Write in specifics: a number, a time, a place, the name of a product.

Set a few rules for your tone, too. Do you address customers formally or casually? Do you use emojis? Which words do you never use? That's your **brand voice**, and it's worth writing down on a single page. The article on [brand voice](@glas-brenda-na-drustvenim-mrezama) has examples and a template.

## How do you know if it's working?

Once a month, look at three numbers: how many people saw your posts (reach), how many reacted to them (engagement) and how many messages or calls came in through social media. Don't check every day, because the numbers jump around from day to day for no reason.

The question at the end of the month is simple: which type of post brought in the most reactions and messages? Do more of that next month. Which metrics make sense and which only look nice is explained in the article on [social media analytics](@analitika-drustvenih-mreza).

## How much time does it take?

A realistic estimate for two networks and three posts a week:

| Task | Time per month |
|---|---|
| Monthly plan | 1 to 2 hours |
| Photos and video | 3 to 4 hours |
| Captions and scheduling | 3 to 4 hours |
| Comments and messages | about 4 hours (10 minutes a day) |
| Reviewing results | 1 hour |

In total, that's **12 to 15 hours a month** when you do it yourself. When someone else does the work, strategy, reporting and back and forth are added, so it comes to 15 to 20 hours. If you don't have that much time, you have three options: pay someone, cut back, or hand part of the work over to a tool. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does a small business have to be on every network?

No. Two networks you run regularly are worth more than five abandoned profiles. Start with the network where your customers are, and add a new one only when the first runs without a struggle.

### Is it better to run social media myself or hire someone?

If you have 12 to 15 hours a month and you enjoy the work, run it yourself. If you don't, hire someone or use a tool that writes and schedules the posts and leaves you only the approval.

### How quickly will I see results?

The first changes in reach show up after a month of regular posting. For messages and customers coming in through social media, count on three to six months, as long as you don't break the rhythm.

### Should I pay for ads?

Not at the start. First build a regular rhythm and see which posts people like. A small ad budget then goes on a post that has already done well, so you take less risk.
