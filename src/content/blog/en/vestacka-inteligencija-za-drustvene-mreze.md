Artificial intelligence today does a good job of **first drafts, adapting one post for several networks, topic suggestions, hashtags and translation**. It does badly at anything that needs knowledge of your business you haven't given it: prices, opening hours, real photos from your shop, and judging a sensitive situation. The best results come from an AI that knows your brand and a person who approves each post before it goes out.

## What does AI do well?

AI does some jobs faster than a person, with no drop in quality. These are jobs where the rule is clear and the content already exists.

The first is **adapting**. You have one piece of news, say new opening hours. Instagram needs a shorter text with a call to action and hashtags, Facebook a more conversational one, LinkedIn a more serious one, X no more than 280 characters, and Google Business Profile something practical with no hashtags. That takes a person twenty minutes and AI a few seconds, and it sticks to each network's rules as it goes.

The second is first drafts. AI doesn't have to write a perfect text. It's enough that it gives you a good starting point you can polish in two minutes, instead of staring at a blank screen.

The third is ideas and planning. Once it knows what you sell and to whom, AI suggests topics for the whole month, spread across your pillars and days. You throw out the five you don't like and the plan is done. What a good plan looks like is covered in the article on [building a content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze).

The fourth is the small, repetitive jobs: hashtags, translation into English, a shorter version for X, a description of a photo for people with visual impairments (alt text), and a summary of the month's results in a few sentences.

## Where does AI get things wrong?

Its biggest weakness is that it doesn't know what you haven't told it. A general chatbot doesn't know that the bakery opens at 6:30, that a croissant costs 180 dinars (RSD) or that you started delivering last week. When you ask it to write a post, it either writes in generalities (“visit us and see the quality for yourself”) or invents a detail that sounds convincing. **A made-up price or the wrong opening hours** on your profile do more damage than a post that never went out.

The second weakness is voice. Without rules, AI writes the way the average marketing text on the internet is written: plenty of adjectives, exclamation marks and stock phrases. The text is grammatically correct, but it sounds like everyone else's. The fix is a written brand voice with examples, which is covered in the article on [brand voice](@glas-brenda-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

The third weakness is sensitive situations. An angry customer, a complaint about quality, a tragedy in town, a political topic. AI can suggest a calm reply, but a person decides whether to respond and how. An automatic reply to a complaint that gets the tone wrong does damage people remember.

The fourth is real photos. AI can make a picture of bread, but it isn't your bread. Customers can tell, and the networks increasingly label content made with artificial intelligence. **A photo taken in your own premises** still works better than any generated one.

## General chatbot or specialised tool?

General AI chatbots are excellent for one-off tasks. The problem starts when you use them to run your social media week after week. ## How do you stop AI making things up?

A few rules apply to any tool:

1. Give it sources: your website, price list, opening hours and a description of your services. The more facts it has, the less it invents.
2. Ask it to write only what the sources say. A good tool does this on its own, but with a general chatbot you have to spell it out.
3. Check numbers, dates, prices and names before every post. It takes thirty seconds.
4. Don't let offers, prices and deadlines go out without a review, even when everything else is automatic.

## Language: Serbian is not English

Many AI tools write Serbian as if it had been translated from English: wrong grammatical cases, word-for-word calques, the formal “Vi” in the wrong places, Latin and Cyrillic script mixed together, and sometimes an English sentence in the middle of the text. Customers notice straight away.

So check how a tool writes in your language before you hand it your profile. Sova writes Serbian in Latin or Cyrillic script, as well as English, and it sticks to one language within a post. Brand details, topics and the call to action are translated, while proper names stay as they are.

## Where should you start?

If you're only just bringing AI in, start with the job that bothers you most. For most small businesses, that's writing posts and adapting them for each network. Let AI write the first drafts for a month while you approve and correct them. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI run my social media completely for me?

It can do most of the work: the plan, the text, visuals from templates, scheduling and reviewing results. Decisions about prices, offers, photos from your shop and replies to complaints should stay with you.

### Do the networks penalise posts written by AI?

There's no rule that penalises a text just because AI wrote it. The networks penalise spam and repetitive content, and posts that give people nothing don't perform well. Generated images may be labelled by the networks as content made with artificial intelligence.

### Is it safe to give AI information about my business?

Give it only what's public anyway or needed for posts: services, prices, opening hours, tone. Before choosing a tool, read its privacy policy and check who processes the data. Sova's is on the [Privacy Policy](/privacy) page.

### Which is better, ChatGPT or a specialised tool?

For an occasional text, a general chatbot is enough. For running several networks regularly, a tool that remembers your brand, adapts the format to each network and publishes for you is better, because it saves the time you'd spend copying and explaining everything again.
