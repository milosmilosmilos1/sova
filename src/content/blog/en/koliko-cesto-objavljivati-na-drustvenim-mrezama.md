For a small business, a good start is **three posts a week on Instagram and Facebook, two on LinkedIn and one a week on Google Business Profile**. TikTok needs more, if you run it at all. What matters more than the exact number is keeping the rhythm going for months: three posts a week for half a year bring in more than daily posts for three weeks.

:::ukratko

## Why does rhythm beat volume?

Social media algorithms track how people react to your posts. A profile that posts regularly gives them more data, and gives the audience a habit. When three weeks of daily posts are followed by a month of silence, people forget you, and your next post starts from zero.

The second reason is quality. When you try to post every day without a plan, after two weeks you start posting just anything. Weak posts don't only do badly on their own. They teach your audience to skip the next one.

## Starting numbers, by network

These are starting values for a small business with one person handling social media for a few hours a week. They aren't a rule. They're a point to start from, and then you measure.

!slika:koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama-info

| Network | Posts a week | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 3 posts and a few stories | stories can go up every day, short and informal |
| Facebook | 3 | same idea as on Instagram, different text |
| LinkedIn | 2 | on weekdays, in the morning |
| TikTok | 3 to 5 | short video, more is better as long as quality holds |
| YouTube Shorts | 2 to 3 | often the same video as the Reel, with a title for YouTube |
| Pinterest | 3 to 5 pins | a pin lives for months, so posting less often still pays off |
| Google Business Profile | 1 | an update, an offer or an event |

You don't have to be on every network. For most small businesses, two or three are enough. How to pick the right ones is explained in the [guide to social media management for small businesses](@vodjenje-drustvenih-mreza-za-mali-biznis).

## How do you work out what you can keep up?

Before you commit to a number, measure how long one post takes you from idea to publishing. For most people it's 30 to 60 minutes when they make posts one at a time, and 15 to 25 minutes when they work in batches.

An example: you have three hours a week. If one post takes 30 minutes, that's six posts a week, say three on Instagram and three on Facebook, with a little time left over for comments. If a post takes an hour, that's three posts, and then you're better off being on one network properly than on two by halves.

What saves the most time is a **monthly plan**, because you're no longer coming up with ideas every day. The process and a template are in the article on [building a content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze).

## Does posting more often lower the reach of each post?

Sometimes, yes. If you post three times on the same day, the posts compete with each other for the same audience. Spread them across the week. On TikTok and in Reels this is less of a problem, because every video gets its own chance with people who don't follow you.

A rule that works well: **one post a day on one network is the upper limit** for a small business on Instagram and Facebook. More than that only makes sense when you really do have a lot of good material, for example during a festival or an opening.

## When is it fine to skip a post?

When you have nothing to say. A “happy Monday” post without a picture from your work doesn't help anyone. It's better to skip the slot and next time post something people want to see.

To make sure this rarely happens, keep a list of ideas in reserve. We have a [list of 40 post ideas](@ideje-za-objave-na-drustvenim-mrezama) that covers most lines of business, and [content pillars](@stubovi-sadrzaja) help too, because every week then has its topics set in advance.

## Is there a penalty for a long break?

There's no rule under which a network punishes a profile for not posting for a month. But the effect is much like a penalty. The people who used to react to your posts have been reacting to other people's in the meantime, so the algorithm links them to other profiles. That's why the first post after a break usually does worse, and the second and third bring your old reach back.

If you know a break is coming, such as your annual holiday, **schedule posts in advance** for those two weeks. One post a week is enough to keep the profile alive.

## How often should you post Stories?

Stories last 24 hours, so people see them as something casual. For a local business they're a good place for small everyday things: what's on offer today, what the queue outside the door looks like, new stock. **Two to five stories a day** is perfectly normal, and you don't need them every day if you have nothing to show.

## What if you have several networks and little time?

Write one idea and adapt it for each network. The same news about a new service goes on Instagram with a photo and a longer caption, on Facebook with shorter text and an invitation to send you a message, and on LinkedIn as a story about why you introduced it. That's one idea and three posts.

## How do you know if the number is right?

After two months, look at two things. Is the reach per post going up or down? Are messages and questions coming in? If reach is falling while you're posting more often, quality has probably dropped. If reach is rising and you have the time, add one more post a week. What else to track is covered in the article on [social media analytics](@analitika-drustvenih-mreza).

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a small business post on Instagram?

Three posts a week and a few stories are a good start. If you keep that up for two months and have time for more, add a fourth post, ideally a short video.

### Is one post a week enough?

For Google Business Profile, yes. For Instagram and Facebook it's the minimum that keeps a profile alive, but it rarely brings growth. If you can only manage one, make it a good one and post it regularly.

### Should I post at weekends?

If your customers are active at weekends, yes. For restaurants, cafés and shops, the weekend often brings more reactions. Services for companies can skip the weekend without losing anything.

### Which is better, more short posts or fewer longer ones?

It depends on the network. On TikTok and in Reels, more short videos usually work better. On LinkedIn and Facebook, one post with real substance is worth more than three empty ones.
