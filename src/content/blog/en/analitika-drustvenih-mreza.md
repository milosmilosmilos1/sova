For a small business, there are **five numbers worth tracking: reach, engagement rate, saves and shares, messages and clicks, and follower growth**. The first three show whether people see and like your content, messages and clicks whether it's leading you to customers, and follower growth whether people want more. Half an hour once a month is enough to see what works and what to change next month.

:::ukratko
- A number only makes sense if it makes you change something.
- **Reach** tells you how many people saw a post, and engagement rate how many of them reacted.
- Saves and shares are the strongest sign that your content is useful.
- Messages, calls and clicks are the closest to money.

## Why don't most numbers help?

Social media insights offer dozens of numbers. Impressions, reach, views, interactions, profile visits, average watch time. There's no shortage of data. The trouble is that most of it doesn't lead to any decision. Likes are going up. So what?

A good rule of thumb: **a number only makes sense if it makes you change something**. If you don't know what you'd do if a number dropped by half, you don't need to track it.

## Five metrics worth tracking

!slika:analitika-drustvenih-mreza-info

Reach is the number of different people who saw a post. It's the basic measure of visibility. Don't confuse it with impressions or views, which count every viewing, so one person can produce several.

Engagement rate tells you what share of the people who saw a post reacted to it. Here's how to calculate it: add up reactions, comments, shares and saves, divide by reach and multiply by a hundred. A post seen by 1,000 people that got 50 reactions has a rate of 5%. **The rate is more useful than the number of likes**, because it lets you compare posts with different reach.

Saves and shares are special reactions. A like is a reflex, but a save means “I'll need this later” and a share means “someone else should see this”. Posts with lots of saves and shares point to your best content pillars.

Messages, calls and clicks are the closest to money. How many messages came in after a post, how many clicks on the link, the phone number or the booking button. This is the number a business owner really wants to know.

Follower growth is a slow metric, but it shows whether people want more. Look at it monthly, not daily. A sudden jump usually comes from a single post or a collaboration, so check which one.

## Numbers that only look good

The total number of followers is the best known of these. A profile with 10,000 followers who never buy anything is worth less than a profile with 800 neighbours who actually come in. The same goes for likes without comments or saves, and for two-second video views.

That doesn't mean these numbers are useless. It means they mustn't be your only goal. An agency that sends you nothing but follower and like counts every month isn't telling you whether your social media is working. ## A 30-minute monthly review

Once a month, sit down with your insights and answer five questions:

1. Which three posts had the biggest reach, and which three the highest engagement rate?
2. Which type of post (carousel, Reels, photo) and which content pillar did best?
3. How many messages, calls and clicks came in, and after which posts?
4. Are reach and engagement going up, holding steady or falling compared with last month?
5. What is the one thing you'll change next month?

**The fifth question is the most important.** Without it, the review is just reading numbers. The answer goes straight into your next [content calendar](@plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze): more of the pillar that works, a different posting time, more short videos.

## Measure by type of post, not post by post

A single post can do very well or very badly for reasons you don't control: rain, a football match, a public holiday. So don't draw conclusions from one post. Group them: all this month's carousels, all the Reels, all the posts from the “behind the scenes” pillar. **The average for a group tells you more than your best post.**

You test posting times the same way, as described in the article on [the best time to post](@najbolje-vreme-za-objavu-na-instagramu), and types of post too. What pillars are and why it's useful to measure by pillar is explained in the article on [content pillars](@stubovi-sadrzaja).

## Where should you keep your numbers?

The networks' insights store the data, but they make it hard to compare month by month. Make a simple spreadsheet: month, reach, average engagement rate, number of messages and calls, number of followers at the end of the month, and one sentence about what you changed. After six months you'll see what really helped and what was chance. And when someone asks whether your social media is working, you'll have an answer in numbers instead of an impression.

## How do you know whether social media brings in customers?

The simplest way is to ask. When a customer comes in or calls, ask how they heard about you, and write it down. After a month you'll have a real picture, without any tools.

For sales through your website, use tagged links (UTM). These are additions to the end of a web address that tell Google Analytics a visitor came from Instagram, from a particular post. Your website statistics will then show how many visits and orders come from social media.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which social media metrics matter most?

For a small business: reach, engagement rate, saves and shares, messages and clicks, and follower growth. Messages and clicks are the closest to sales.

### How do you calculate engagement rate?

Add up reactions, comments, shares and saves, divide by the post's reach and multiply by a hundred. For example, 50 reactions on a reach of 1,000 people is 5%.

### How often should I look at the statistics?

Once a month for a proper review and decisions. Looking every day leads you to draw conclusions from random rises and falls.

### Which is better, reach or engagement?

Both, together. Reach tells you whether people see you, engagement whether they like what they see. A post with high reach and low engagement is a missed opportunity.
