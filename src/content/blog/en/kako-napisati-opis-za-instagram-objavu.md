A good Instagram caption has four parts: **a first line that stops the scroll, specific content, one clear call to action and up to five hashtags**. The first line matters most, because under the photo Instagram shows only the start of the text, and the rest sits behind “more”. If the opening doesn't catch people's interest, nobody will read the rest.

:::ukratko
- The first line does most of the work: a promise, a question or a surprising fact.
- Specific beats general, so write **a number, a time, a place and the name of a product**.
- One call to action per post, not three.
- Since December 2025, Instagram allows a maximum of five hashtags per post.
- A caption can be up to 2,200 characters long, but the length should follow the content.
:::

## Why does the first line matter so much?

On a phone screen, below the post you see the profile name and roughly the first two sentences of the caption. Everything else is hidden behind “more”. People decide whether to tap in less than a second, looking at the picture and that opening.

So the first line can't be “Good morning, dear followers!” or “New on the menu!”. It has to tell people why they should keep reading. Three forms that work well:

- a promise of something useful: “Three mistakes that make hair colour fade faster.”
- a question that hits home: “Do you know why homemade bread goes stale by the next day?”
- a surprising fact: “Each of our croissants takes 36 hours to make.”

## Four parts of a good caption

!slika:kako-napisati-opis-za-instagram-objavu-info

After the hook comes the content. The rule here is simple: **specific beats general**. “Quality products at affordable prices” means nothing, because everyone writes it. “Croissants made with French butter, 180 dinars, every morning from 7:00” tells you exactly what you get.

The third part is the call to action. One, clear, and with a way to do it: “Book an appointment by message”, “Save this post for your next trip to the shop”, “Tell us in the comments which colour you'd pick”. Three calls to action in the same post confuse the reader, so they end up doing nothing.

Hashtags go at the end. Since December 2025, Instagram has allowed **a maximum of five hashtags** per post and per Reel. That's a good thing, because it matters more that they're accurate than that there are lots of them. How to choose them is covered in the article on [Instagram hashtags](@hashtagovi-na-instagramu).

## Before and after examples

Three real types of post, rewritten using the rules above.

| Business | Before | After (first line) |
|---|---|---|
| Bakery | “Fresh bread every day! Come and visit us.” | “Yeast bread that keeps for three days without going rubbery.” |
| Hair salon | “New treatment available, ask us for details.” | “Hair dry after colouring? Here's what we're doing differently from 1 November.” |
| Accountant | “A reminder to our clients about deadlines.” | “The filing deadline is the 15th. Two things sole traders forget every single time.” |

You can see the pattern: a specific thing, a specific problem or a specific date. Anyone can write an opening like that, but you need to know what's bothering your customer.

## How long should a caption be?

Instagram allows up to 2,200 characters. That doesn't mean you should use them all. The length depends on the content:

- Short, one to three sentences, when the picture says it all: a new dish, a new haircut, a photo of your space.
- Medium, five to ten sentences, when you're explaining something: how to use a product, what a service includes.
- Long, when you're telling a story or giving advice in steps. Then split the text into short paragraphs with a blank line between them.

**Nobody reads a long block of text on a phone.** Short paragraphs, one thought each, are easy to read.

## Emojis: yes or no?

It depends on your tone. A café for students can use them more freely, a law firm more carefully. If you use them, let them replace or emphasise a word rather than sit at the end of every sentence. One to three per caption is usually enough. Your tone rules are worth writing down once, for all your posts. That's what the article on [brand voice](@glas-brenda-na-drustvenim-mrezama) is about.

## Words people search for

Instagram search reads the caption text too, not just the hashtags. If you're writing about children's haircuts in Zemun (a part of Belgrade), make sure the caption says “children's haircuts” and “Zemun”, naturally, in a sentence. That helps people looking for exactly that to find you. The same goes for posts about products: use the words your customers use, not your internal names.

## Captions for Reels, carousels and stories

With Reels, the caption matters less, because people are watching the video. The first line should confirm what the video promises, and the text on the video itself carries the message. With carousels, the caption says what's on the slides and why it's worth swiping to the end. With stories, the text is at its shortest: one sentence on the image itself and a sticker with a question, a poll or a link, because people only look at a story for a few seconds. The articles on [carousels](@instagram-karusel) and [Reels ideas](@ideje-za-reels-za-lokalni-biznis) cover carousels and Reels in detail.

## Checking before you post

Before you tap “Share”, read the caption out loud. If you stumble over a sentence, so will the reader. Then check four things. Does the first line give people a reason to tap “more”? Does the text contain at least one specific fact, number or name? Is there one clear call to action? Are the hashtags accurate?

Finally, look at the post through the eyes of a customer who doesn't know you. Do they know what you sell, where you are and how to contact you? **If they don't, add one sentence.** That's usually all that's missing for a post to bring in a message and not just a like.

You're allowed to edit a caption after posting. If you spot a mistake in a price or in your opening hours, fix it straight away, because wrong information does more harm than a correction.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many characters can an Instagram caption have?

Up to 2,200. The preview shows only the beginning, roughly the first two sentences, so put the most important part first.

### How many hashtags should I use?

Five at most, because that's the limit Instagram introduced in December 2025. Choose the ones that describe the post and the location accurately, not the most popular ones.

### Do hashtags go in the caption or in a comment?

In the caption. That way they're part of the post from the very first moment and search sees them straight away. Putting them in the first comment has no real advantage.

### Is a short caption better than a long one?

Neither is better in itself. Short when the picture says it all, longer when you're explaining something or telling a story. What matters is that the first line catches people's interest.
