A content calendar is **a table that gives the date, network, type of post, topic and call to action for every slot in the month**. You make it once a month, in an hour or two, and after that every post takes half as long, because you're not coming up with an idea on the spot. You don't need any special software to start: a spreadsheet or a notebook is enough.

:::ukratko
- Make the plan once a month, not every morning.
- **Seven columns** are enough: date and time, network, type, topic, pillar, call to action, status.
- Put in the month's important dates first, and only then the regular posts.
- Leave one or two free slots a week for whatever comes up along the way.
- The plan isn't set in stone: if something important happens, move a post.
:::

## Why does a plan save so much time?

Without a plan, every post starts with the question “what do I post today?”. That question takes more time than the writing itself. On top of that, when you come up with something in a hurry, you usually post the same thing as last week, and the profile becomes monotonous.

With a plan, you work in batches. You decide on topics once, shoot once, write once. Your brain isn't constantly jumping between tasks, so everything goes faster.

## Which columns does the plan need?

Many templates on the internet have twenty columns. Most small businesses need seven:

| Column | What you enter | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Date and time | when it goes out | Tuesday 3 November at 19:00 |
| Network | where it goes out | Instagram |
| Type | the format of the post | carousel, Reel, photo, story |
| Topic | what it's about, in one sentence | five haircuts for short hair |
| Pillar | which group of topics it belongs to | tips |
| Call to action | what you want people to do | book an appointment by message |
| Status | how far you've got | idea, written, scheduled |

The “pillar” column shows you whether your content is balanced, so that it isn't all selling. What pillars are and how to set them is covered in the article on [content pillars](@stubovi-sadrzaja).

## Five steps to a monthly plan

!slika:plan-objava-za-drustvene-mreze-info

1. Write down the month's goal in one sentence. For example, “ten new clients for keratin treatment” or “people should know we're open on Sundays too”.
2. Put in the important dates: holidays, promotions, seasonal events, anniversaries, new products. They get their slots first.
3. Set the schedule: how many posts a week, on which networks and on which days. If you don't know how many is enough, see the article on [how often to post](@koliko-cesto-objavljivati-na-drustvenim-mrezama).
4. For each slot, write the topic in one sentence. Alternate the pillars and the types of post.
5. Write the captions in one or two sittings and **schedule them straight away**.

Once you get used to it, the first four steps take **an hour**. The fifth step takes longer, but you can spread it across the weeks.

## Where do you get topics when you have none?

The best source is your customers' questions. Anything people ask you at the till, on the phone or in messages is a topic for a post, because you know someone is interested in it. Keep a note on your phone and add questions as they come in. After a month you'll have more topics than slots.

The second source is your best old posts. Something that did well six months ago can go out again, with a new photo and slightly different text. The third source is the season: what people in your line of business look for in November, and what they look for in May. **A bakery in December** talks about slava cakes (slava is the family saint's day that many Serbian families celebrate) and gift boxes, and in June about cold drinks and breakfast outdoors.

## An example week for a hair salon

A salon in Novi Sad, on Instagram and Facebook, three posts a week plus stories. The month's goal: fill the appointments on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, when it's quietest.

| Day | Network | Type | Topic | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday 19:00 | Instagram and Facebook | carousel | five hairstyles that last until your next cut | save it for next time |
| Wednesday 12:00 | Instagram | Reel | 30 seconds of before and after, colouring | book an appointment by message |
| Friday 18:00 | Instagram and Facebook | photo | Milica, the new hairdresser on the team | welcome her in the comments |
| Every day | Instagram | stories | free appointments for today | send us a message |

Notice how tips, work and people take turns. **There is a sales call, but it isn't in every post.** You'll find more ideas for each line of business in the [list of 40 post ideas](@ideje-za-objave-na-drustvenim-mrezama).

## Where should you keep the plan?

For one person, a spreadsheet in Excel or Google Sheets is enough. For a team of two or three, a shared calendar is useful, so everyone can see what's scheduled and who's doing what.

The problem with a spreadsheet is that it doesn't publish anything. You still have to copy every post into the network or into a scheduling tool, and that's where half the saving gets lost. Scheduling options are described in the article on [scheduling posts on Instagram and Facebook](@zakazivanje-objava-na-instagramu-i-facebooku).

## The most common mistakes in a plan

- A plan with no goal for the month, so the posts look nice but lead nowhere.
- Only sales posts. People follow profiles that give them something, not ones that only sell.
- Too many posts in the plan. It's better to plan nine posts you'll actually publish than twenty, half of which never go out.
- No free slots. When something nice happens in the shop, there's nowhere to put it.
- Nobody looks at the plan once the month is over. At the end of the month, check which topics did best and repeat them. How to do that is covered in the article on [analytics](@analitika-drustvenih-mreza).

## Frequently asked questions

### How far ahead should you make a content calendar?

A month ahead is a good measure for most small businesses. For seasonal work, before the holidays for example, it helps to have a rough plan for two to three months and to work out the details a month before it starts.

### Does the plan have to be the same for every network?

No. The same topic can go on several networks, but the type of post, the text and the timing are adapted. On LinkedIn a story works better, on Instagram a carousel or a short video.

### What if something important happens in the middle of the month?

Move the planned post and publish the news. That's why it's a good idea to leave one or two free slots a week in your plan.

### Can AI make a content calendar?

It can make a good suggestion, especially when it knows your brand, your offer and your tone. The final choice of topics and dates should stay with you, because only you know what's really going to happen in the shop next month.
