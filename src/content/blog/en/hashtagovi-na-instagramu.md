Since December 2025, Instagram has allowed **a maximum of five hashtags per post and per Reel**. Accurate hashtags that describe the post and the place work best: for example one local tag (#novisad), one for the topic of the post (#kroasan, Serbian for croissant), one for the type of business (#pekara, bakery) and one of your own (#pekarazrno). These days hashtags help Instagram understand what a post is about. They are no longer the main source of reach.

## What changed in December 2025?

Instagram announced that it was gradually cutting the number of hashtags you can put in the caption of a post or Reel to five. Before that the limit was 30, and many profiles used every last slot, with a string of generic tags that had nothing to do with the post. The change was reported by many sites, for example [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/).

Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, had said before that hashtags don't increase visibility, but that they help sort a post and connect it with similar posts. The limit of five only confirmed what was already true: five accurate tags are worth more than thirty random ones.

## How do you choose the right five?

!slika:hashtagovi-na-instagramu-info

For a local business, this formula works well:

1. One local hashtag: your town or part of town (#novisad, #zemun, #vracar).
2. One or two topical ones: what's in the post (#kroasan, #balayage, #knjigovodstvo).
3. One for the type of business or service (#pekara, #frizerskisalon).
4. One of your own, with your brand name (#pekarazrno), if people use it.

That's five. If you don't have enough good ones, use three. **Fewer accurate tags are better than more wrong ones.**

The examples are in Serbian because the customers of a business in Serbia search in Serbian: #knjigovodstvo means bookkeeping and #frizerskisalon means hair salon. Use the language your own customers search in.

## Which hashtags should you avoid?

- Generic, huge tags (#love, #instagood, #photooftheday). A post gets lost in them within seconds and never reaches the people who would buy.
- Tags that have nothing to do with the post. Instagram may treat them as an attempt to cheat the system and show the post to fewer people.
- The same string of five hashtags on every post. Adapt them to the topic.
- Tags in a foreign language when your customers are local. For a bakery in Serbia, #bakery can bring in followers from all over the world, but not customers to your street.

Before you use a new hashtag, look at what's posted under it. If you see spam or posts that have nothing to do with the topic, skip it.

## Do hashtags still affect reach?

A little. These days Instagram decides who sees a post mainly based on **how people react to it** and what they like to watch. Hashtags help the system understand the topic, especially for new profiles it doesn't know much about yet. But if the post is weak, a hashtag won't save it.

So it's smarter to put your time into the first line of the caption and into the picture. How to write a caption people actually read is explained in the article on [how to write an Instagram caption](@kako-napisati-opis-za-instagram-objavu).

## Words in the caption instead of hashtags

Instagram search reads the caption text, and the text on the image itself if there is any. If you write “children's haircuts in Zemun, Saturdays from 9:00”, the post can show up for people searching for “children's haircuts Zemun”, even without a hashtag. **Write in the words customers use** when they search, not in the internal names of your services.

## Hashtags on other networks

The rules aren't the same everywhere. These are rough guidelines for each network, for a local business:

| Network | How many hashtags | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | up to 5 | accurate, local and topical |
| Facebook | 0 to 1 | they don't help much, the text matters more |
| LinkedIn | 2 to 3 | profession and field, at the end of the text |
| TikTok | 3 to 5 | topic and niche, plus words in the caption |
| YouTube Shorts | 1 to 2 | #Shorts and the topic |
| Pinterest | no hashtags | keywords in the pin title and description |
| Google Business Profile | no hashtags | practical information |

## A branded hashtag: is it worth it?

It is when other people use it too, not just you. For example, customers who post photos of your product, or people who come to an event you organise. Then it becomes the place where their posts are collected. If you're the only one using it, it does no harm, but it doesn't bring much either. Ask customers to use it, for instance with a small giveaway or by printing it on the receipt.

## How do you check whether a hashtag works?

In a post's insights, Instagram shows where people came from: the home feed, your profile, search or Explore. If the share from search and hashtags is small, that doesn't mean you should drop them. It means the post got its reach another way, which is to be expected.

A more useful test is a comparison. For two months, use the same local hashtag on half your posts and leave it off the other half. If there's a difference in reach from search, it will show after about twenty posts. If there isn't, **don't waste time hunting for the perfect tags**. Put that time into the picture and the first line.

Record the results for each post in the same spreadsheet where you track your other numbers. What else is worth recording is explained in the article on [social media analytics](@analitika-drustvenih-mreza). ## Frequently asked questions

### How many hashtags can I use on Instagram?

Five at most per post and per Reel. Instagram started introducing this limit in December 2025, gradually, for all accounts.

### Do hashtags increase the number of followers?

Rarely, and only a little. They can help Instagram sort new profiles into the right topic. Followers come from good posts that people share and save.

### Where do hashtags go, in the caption or in a comment?

In the caption, at the end of the text. That way they're part of the post from the first moment. The first comment has no real advantage.

### Should I use hashtags in English?

Only if some of your customers are in English-speaking countries too. For a local business in Serbia, hashtags in Serbian, with the name of your town, work better.
